# Executive Audience Lenses

Use this reference after extracting the facts and before reframing the update. A lens changes information priority, follow-up questions, risks, and asks. It never changes the underlying facts.

## Lens selection

1. If the user names the audience, use that lens.
2. If the audience is not named, infer only when the context makes one role unambiguous.
3. Otherwise default to **Direct Manager** and state `Audience lens: Direct Manager (default)` outside the copy-ready version.
4. If the update serves multiple leaders, ask for one primary audience only when choosing the wrong lens would materially change the update. Otherwise use the closest lens and note the assumption.
5. Never blend all lenses. A report optimized for everyone is prioritized for no one.

## CEO

**Prioritize:** strategic alignment, company-level growth, resource allocation, major opportunity cost, decisions that are difficult to reverse.

**Deprioritize:** implementation detail unless it changes strategy, timing, cost, or organizational risk.

**Likely questions:**
- Which company priority does this advance?
- What is the expected growth or strategic upside?
- What are we choosing not to fund or pursue?
- What decision or resource shift is needed now?

**Shape the ask around:** strategic choice, investment level, priority, or cross-company alignment.

## CPO

**Prioritize:** user value, product judgment, evidence quality, learning velocity, adoption, retention, and whether the team is solving the right problem.

**Deprioritize:** shipping volume without user learning or behavior change.

**Likely questions:**
- What user problem did we validate?
- What evidence changed our product judgment?
- What did we learn, and how quickly?
- Which behavior or product metric should move next?

**Shape the ask around:** product principle, user segment, experiment direction, or product tradeoff.

## CTO

**Prioritize:** technical risk, architecture, security and reliability, dependencies, operational cost, scalability, reversibility, and engineering capacity.

**Deprioritize:** polished product claims that omit technical feasibility or production risk.

**Likely questions:**
- What can fail in production, and how will we detect it?
- Which dependency or architectural constraint is on the critical path?
- Will this scale in performance, cost, and team ownership?
- Is the decision reversible, and what is the mitigation plan?

**Shape the ask around:** architecture decision, engineering capacity, dependency ownership, risk acceptance, or sequencing.

## VP Design

**Prioritize:** user insight, experience quality, clarity of design rationale, quality bar, system consistency, cross-functional alignment, and how design reduced ambiguity.

**Deprioritize:** number of screens or design iterations without evidence, decision, or implementation movement.

**Likely questions:**
- Which user insight drove this decision?
- How do we know the experience is better?
- What quality bar or design principle are we protecting?
- Where is cross-functional alignment still weak?

**Shape the ask around:** quality tradeoff, research need, design direction, staffing, or Product/Engineering alignment.

## VP Product

**Prioritize:** product direction, portfolio fit, customer and market signal, prioritization, roadmap consequence, measurable product outcomes, and speed to validation.

**Deprioritize:** feature-level detail that does not affect direction, sequencing, adoption, or differentiation.

**Likely questions:**
- Does this strengthen or challenge our product direction?
- Why this problem and why now?
- What customer or market evidence supports the priority?
- What changes in the roadmap based on what we learned?

**Shape the ask around:** product direction, roadmap priority, target segment, sequencing, or investment tradeoff.

## Direct Manager

**Prioritize:** execution progress, commitments, delivery risk, ownership, dependencies, decision quality, and where the individual needs coaching or support.

**Deprioritize:** broad strategy language that obscures what actually moved and who owns the next step.

**Likely questions:**
- Are we on track against the commitment?
- What is blocked, and what have you already tried?
- What do you own, and what depends on others?
- What support or decision do you need from me?

**Shape the ask around:** unblock, scope, priority, feedback, coordination, or coaching.

## Output rule

The selected lens controls emphasis, not truth. Never invent role-specific impact. If the lens calls for evidence the user did not provide, mark it as a gap in `Manager Questions` or the follow-up questions instead of asserting it in the update.
